# unfold — grounding a paper, book, specification, or long document

Read when the subject is text. The layers follow the document's own structure, and brevity
never replaces information: shorten by choosing the layer, not by dropping what it says.

## Layers by granularity

`overview or table of contents → chapter or section → paragraph → sentence or term`

- **Skeleton:** read the title, abstract or introduction, headings, and conclusion before
  anything else. Report the question the document asks, the claim it makes, and its map.
- **Chapter or section:** one per turn, as the learner's questions pull. State what it
  contributes to the whole before its detail.
- **Paragraph and sentence:** descend only on request or where a claim carries weight;
  quote the passage and explain it in plain words.

For a paper, useful layers are the claim, the method, the evidence, and the stated limits.
For a book, go chapter by chapter, tying each to the through-line. For a specification,
follow one concrete case through the rules before the rules in general.

## Evidence

Cite the section, page, or quoted passage for every key claim. Keep three things apart:

- **the author says** — in the text, quoted or tightly paraphrased;
- **my reading** — your inference from the text, labeled as such;
- **not in the text** — outside knowledge, or a gap the document leaves open.

Never present a summary as if it were the author's wording. If you have only part of the
document (an excerpt, a chapter), say which part and do not generalize beyond it.
